1 · Verdict: merge-quality evidence for the frozen snapshot

  • Learning behavior reproduced. Pure-RL reward differs by at most 0.006 in the three matched windows. OPD reverse-KL agrees after the cold-start window: 0.1246 vs 0.1242 at steps 50–100 and 0.0954 vs 0.0958 at steps 150–199.
  • The fully async path improved. Wall-clock rollout time is 9–14% lower and effective token throughput is 15–26% higher, depending on the matched window.
  • The OPD path is slower for an understood reason. Upstream's bridge now honors full activation recomputation. The additional trainer work is hidden by sampler wait in the async pipeline, but passes directly into OPD step time.
  • The successful runs are stable. The post-sync async run reached step 2034 over 22h43m and was stopped by decision; the OPD baseline completed 200/200 steps.
  • One estimator risk remains visible. Sampled RKLD injects an unclamped per-token k1 into advantages. Global gradient clipping contained the early spike, but it is the only source-level safety backstop in this recipe.

5 · OPD: stable-window reverse-KL parity, with a separate trainer-side DAgger term

The hybrid OPD baseline optimizes two distinct terms. Sampled RKLD uses the rollout action and becomes a detached advantage coefficient. Top-K+Rest DAgger uses the teacher's sparse distribution and produces a direct trainer-side cross-entropy gradient. Task reward is logged but does not enter this recipe's optimization objective.

6 · Performance mechanism: recomputation is now honored, not newly inefficient

Both recipes already requested full activation recomputation. Before the sync, the Megatron-Bridge path silently dropped those flags. Upstream's bridge runtime configuration now propagates them, so the post-sync actor re-runs each layer's forward immediately before its backward. That trades memory headroom for approximately one extra forward-equivalent of work.

Operational decision. Keep recomputation enabled for the validated baseline. Removing it could reclaim roughly 25% of actor speed, but costs the memory headroom the flags were intended to provide. It yields little end-to-end benefit when async sampling is the bottleneck; it matters more for the synchronous OPD cadence and should be a separate controlled experiment.

7 · Fully async admission: the startup spike is a cohort effect

accepted_staleness describes only groups admitted into the training batch. over_cap_ratio measures completed attempts rejected by the cap and recycled for regeneration. A high rejection ratio therefore signals discarded sampler work, not an oversized or stale train batch.

The gate does not change per-step training compute: every accepted batch still contains the configured number of groups under the same token budget. During the startup wave, ready supply is abundant, so trainer wait remains low even while sampler decode is discarded. This is why the spike is not visible in actor timing.

8 · OPD gradient spike: contained, reproducible, and correctly attributed

The current recipe uses rollout logprobs as q_old. At cold start, some sampled tokens are tens of nats more likely under the student than under the teacher. That heavy-tailed sampled k1 enters advantages without clamping. PPO's ratio clip does not cap advantage magnitude; global gradient clipping is the final backstop.

AtOPD = Attask − β [log qold(yt|ht) − log pT(yt|ht)]
In this baseline, task reward is telemetry-only, β=1, and advantage normalization is disabled. The bracketed term is detached before it scales the sampled-token policy gradient.

8.1 What the current evidence licenses

Current recipe: benign cold-start transient under rollout q_old, staleness cap 2, and global gradient clipping.
Wider-staleness direction: not cleared by this result. Earlier recompute-q_old, cap-4 evidence showed chronic spike clusters and negative RKLD.
Hardening order: if that direction resumes, measure the k1 distribution, then test per-token winsorization and an RKLD coefficient warm-up as explicit algorithm changes. Do not rewrite the frozen baseline.
